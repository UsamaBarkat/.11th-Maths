Hybridization

Chapter 3 · Lesson 4 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 16–18 min read Topic 3.2.2

🎯 Why Should You Care?

Here is a problem that should keep you awake. Count carbon's unpaired electrons: 1s2, 2s2, 2px1, 2py1. That is two unpaired electrons, so carbon's valency should be 2.

But every carbon compound you have ever seen — CH4, CO2, diamond, sugar, you — has carbon forming four bonds. The rule is simply wrong for carbon. It is also wrong for beryllium and boron.

Linus Pauling's fix was hybridization: orbitals of nearly equal energy mix into new, identical orbitals. It repairs the valency problem and explains every bond angle VSEPR predicted in the last lesson — this time from the orbitals themselves.

📖 The Problem: Valency That Doesn't Add Up

Valency is generally regarded as the number of unpaired electrons in the valence shell of an atom. However, this rule is disregarded in some cases such as Beryllium, Boron and Carbon.

ElementValency by the unpaired-electron ruleActual valency
Be0 — would behave as an inert gas2 (divalent)
B1 — mono valent3 (trivalent)
C2 — divalent4 (tetravalent)

The fix, part 1 — promotion to the excited state

To account for the valency of such elements, it is assumed that some of the paired electrons are uncoupled and promoted to an orbital of slightly higher energy within the same orbit, before the atoms form chemical bonds.

The promotion will require an input of energy, but such energy will be available from the heat of reaction when additional covalent bonds are formed. The arrangement of electrons after promotion is referred to as an excited state.

The fix, part 2 — mixing the orbitals

Linus Pauling settled this disparity by suggesting the idea that atomic orbitals having nearly equal energies can be mixed in various ways within an atom to form equivalent hybrid orbitals.

📚 Definition

"The mixing of different atomic orbitals to produce the same number of equivalent orbitals, having the same shape and energy, is known as hybridization."

The orbitals so formed are called hybrid orbitals and are designated according to the number of mixing orbitals.

🔑
Two rules that are never broken

1. The number of hybrid orbitals produced always equals the number of atomic orbitals mixed. Mix 4, get 4.
2. All hybrid orbitals are equivalent — same shape, same energy. That equivalence is exactly why the bond angles come out symmetrical.

Do You Know? — why s-character matters

s-character in sp3, sp2 and sp hybridization is 25%, 33% and 50% respectively. The greater the s-character, the closer the electron is to the nucleus and the stronger the bond. Thus an sp hybridized orbital forms a relatively stronger bond.

🔺 sp3 Hybridization

📚 Definition

"Combination of one s and three p orbitals to produce four sp3 hybrid orbitals is known as sp3 or tetrahedral hybridization."

The hybrid orbitals are different from the pure s or p orbitals; they possess the character of both s and p orbitals in the ratio of 1:3. These sp3 orbitals are directed towards the four corners of a regular tetrahedron, in which each angle is 109.5°.

Methane Molecule (CH4)

The electronic configuration of carbon is (1s2, 2s2, 2px1, 2py1, 2pz0), but it is assumed that one electron of the 2s orbital gets promoted to the 2pz orbital to make it tetravalent:

C (z = 6): 1s2, 2s1, 2px1, 2py1, 2pz1  (tetravalent)

In methane, the four atomic orbitals of carbon undergo a mixing process to produce four new orbitals of equal energy and shape, each named an sp3 hybrid orbital.

All four sp3 hybrid orbitals are then directed towards the corners of a regular tetrahedron at an angle of 109.5°. Since each sp3 hybrid orbital has one electron, it overlaps with the 1s orbital of a hydrogen atom. Thus four C–H sigma bonds are formed.

Ammonia Molecule (NH3)

The electronic configuration of nitrogen is 1s2, 2s2, 2px1, 2py1, 2pz1. Thus according to the concept of hybridization, one s and three p orbitals of the valence shell of nitrogen undergo the process of mixing to give four sp3 hybrid orbitals, directed towards the four corners of a tetrahedron.

Out of the four hybrid orbitals, three have single electrons, hence they overlap with the s-orbital of hydrogen and form three N–H sigma bonds. The fourth sp3 orbital, which has a lone pair of electrons, remains unbonded. The stronger repulsion of this orbital deviates the bond angle from 109.5° to 107° and gives rise to a pyramidal geometry.

Water Molecule (H2O)

The electronic configuration of oxygen is 1s2, 2s2, 2px2, 2py1, 2pz1. The four outer-shell orbitals are assumed to be mixed together, which results in the formation of four new sp3 hybrid orbitals directed at the corners of a tetrahedron.

Since only two sp3 orbitals possess single electrons, they overlap with the s-orbital of hydrogen to form two O–H sigma bonds. The remaining two sp3 hybrid orbitals contain lone pairs of electrons.

Because the repulsion of a lone pair is greater than a bond pair, the shape of the water molecule is not a regular tetrahedron. The distortion of the bond angle from 109.5° to 104° makes an angular geometry.

🔗
Same three molecules, same three angles as Lesson 3

CH4 109.5°, NH3 107°, H2O ~104.5° — VSEPR predicted these by counting electron pairs; hybridization explains them through the orbitals. Two theories, one answer. That agreement is why both are taught.

🔻 sp2 Hybridization

📚 Definition

"The mixing of one s and two p orbitals to produce three sp2 hybrid orbitals is referred to as sp2 (trigonal hybridization)."

  • These orbitals are co-planar and directed towards the corners of an equilateral triangle (trigonal) at an angle of 120° to each other.
  • sp2 orbitals are different from either s or p orbitals and are considered to have a character that is one third s and two thirds p.
  • The fourth p atomic orbital, which is not involved in hybridization, remains unhybridized at right angles to the plane of the sp2 orbitals.

sp2 hybridization provides a good model for describing the geometry of molecules which contain carbon bonded to only three other atoms or groups, such as ethylene (C2H4) or boron trifluoride (BF3).

Boron promotes one 2s electron to become trivalent

One half filled s orbital and two half filled p orbitals undergo the process of hybridization to produce three sp2 hybrid orbitals. These three sp2 hybrid orbitals lie in the same plane (coplanar) with an angle of 120° and make a planar triangular geometry.

These three sp2 orbitals of boron then overlap with three p orbitals of different fluorine atoms.

Ethene Molecule (C2H4)

The ethene molecule consists of two central carbon atoms. Each carbon possesses three sp2 hybrid orbitals and one unhybrid pz orbital.

  • These three sp2 hybrid orbitals arrange themselves in triangular planar geometry at a 120° angle.
  • Two sp2 orbitals of each carbon overlap with the s orbitals of two hydrogen atoms to form two C–H sigma bonds.
  • The third sp2 orbital of both carbon atoms overlaps linearly with each other to form a C–C sigma bond.
  • The unhybrid pz orbitals of both carbon atoms, which lie at right angles, overlap side-wise to form a pi bond.
C2H4 = 5 sigma bonds (4 C–H + 1 C–C) + 1 pi bond

➖ sp Hybridization

📚 Definition

The hybridization of one s and one p atomic orbital leads to two hybrid orbitals known as sp hybrid orbitals.

  • These orbitals are co-linear at an angle of 180°, which provides maximum separation and overlap.
  • Each sp hybrid orbital has 50% s character and 50% p character (1:1 ratio).
  • The other two p orbitals remain unhybridized and are oriented at right angles to each other and to the plane of the hybrid orbitals.

Ethyne Molecule (C2H2)

The type of hybridization in each carbon atom of ethyne is sp, where only s and px orbitals are involved in the mixing process, while the py and pz orbitals remain unhybridized.

The two sp orbitals are inclined to each other at 180°, whereas the two unhybrid orbitals (py and pz) lie at right angles to the hybrid orbitals as well as to each other. The two hybrid orbitals (sp, sp) and the two atomic orbitals (py and pz) contain single electrons.

  • Each carbon uses one sp orbital to overlap with the s orbital of hydrogen, while the other sp orbital is involved in sigma bonding between the two carbon atoms. Thus overall three sigma bonds are formed at 180° angles, which makes a linear geometry.
  • The unused p orbitals lying parallel to each other undergo side-wise overlapping to form two pi bonds.
  • One sigma and two pi bonds between the carbon atoms is identified as a triple bond.

Beryllium Chloride (BeCl2)

The excited-state electronic configuration of beryllium is 1s2, 2s1, 2px1, 2py, 2pz, which indicates that the 2s and 2px orbitals of beryllium should be involved in the mixing process. Hence the result is the production of two sp hybrid orbitals.

Two sp hybrid orbitals of beryllium overlap with the p orbitals of two chlorine atoms in a straight line at a 180° angle. The linear geometry of the molecule is due to the maximum repulsion of two electron pairs.

The fastest shortcut in the chapter

Count the active electron pairs (bond pairs + lone pairs) on the central atom: 4 → sp3, 3 → sp2, 2 → sp. That single count gives you the hybridization and the geometry in one step.

💡 Real-Life Way to Think About It

  1. Mixing paint 🎨 — Mix one tin of white with three of blue and you get four tins of an identical new shade. You cannot get five tins, and no tin is still pure white. That is hybridization: same number in, same number out, all identical.
  2. Climbing a small step to jump higher 🪜 — Promotion costs energy, but the atom gets it back many times over from the extra bonds it can now form. Carbon spends a little to make four bonds instead of two.
  3. An umbrella 🌂 — NH3 is a tetrahedron with one corner replaced by an invisible lone pair, so what you see is a pyramid — like an umbrella with the handle pushing down on the fabric.
  4. Holding hands closer to the centre 🤲 — More s-character keeps electrons nearer the nucleus, so the bond is stronger. sp (50% s) beats sp2 (33%) beats sp3 (25%).

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "sp3 means three orbitals mixed."

❌ The superscript counts the p orbitals only. sp3 = one s + three p = four orbitals mixed → four hybrid orbitals. Likewise sp2 mixes three and gives three; sp mixes two and gives two.

⚠️ "NH3 and H2O are not sp3 because they aren't tetrahedral."

❌ Both are sp3 hybridized — four orbitals mixed. What changes is how many hybrid orbitals hold lone pairs instead of bonds. The hybridization is tetrahedral; the visible shape is pyramidal or angular.

⚠️ "All the p orbitals get used up in sp2 and sp."

❌ In sp2 one p orbital stays unhybridized, at right angles to the plane — and it is what forms the pi bond in C2H4. In sp, two p orbitals stay unhybridized and form the two pi bonds in C2H2.

⚠️ "Hybrid orbitals form pi bonds."

❌ Hybrid orbitals form sigma bonds only. Pi bonds are always formed by the leftover unhybridized p orbitals.

⚠️ "Promotion happens for every element."

❌ The book applies promotion to the elements where the simple valency rule fails — Be, B and C. Nitrogen and oxygen already have enough unpaired electrons; they hybridize without needing promotion.

Short Questions

Q6. Define hybridization and state its two basic rules.

Answer: "The mixing of different atomic orbitals to produce the same number of equivalent orbitals, having the same shape and energy, is known as hybridization." The two rules: (1) the number of hybrid orbitals equals the number of atomic orbitals mixed, and (2) all hybrid orbitals are equivalent in shape and energy.

Q7. Why does carbon show a valency of 4 when it has only 2 unpaired electrons?

Answer: In the ground state, carbon is 1s2 2s2 2px1 2py1 — two unpaired electrons. One paired electron of the 2s orbital is uncoupled and promoted to the empty 2pz orbital, giving the excited state 1s2 2s1 2px1 2py1 2pz1 with four unpaired electrons. The energy needed for promotion is recovered from the heat of reaction when the extra covalent bonds form.

Q8. Which hybridization would you expect for the central atom in BeCl2, BF3, CH4 and C2H2?

Answer: BeCl2 → sp (linear, 180°) · BF3 → sp2 (planar triangular, 120°) · CH4 → sp3 (tetrahedral, 109.5°) · C2H2 → sp (linear, 180°).

Q9. Explain sp2 hybridization in the C2H4 molecule.

Answer: Each carbon in ethene has three sp2 hybrid orbitals and one unhybrid pz orbital. The three sp2 orbitals arrange in triangular planar geometry at 120°. Two of them overlap with the s orbitals of two hydrogens to give two C–H sigma bonds; the third overlaps linearly with the third sp2 orbital of the other carbon to give the C–C sigma bond. The two unhybrid pz orbitals, lying at right angles, overlap side-wise to form a pi bond. Total: 5 sigma + 1 pi.

📝 Summary

6 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. The unpaired-electron rule for valency fails for Be, B and C. It is fixed by promotion to an excited state, paid for by the heat of reaction.
  2. Linus Pauling: hybridization is the mixing of atomic orbitals of nearly equal energy to give the same number of equivalent orbitals of identical shape and energy.
  3. sp3 — one s + three p → four orbitals, 109.5°, 25% s. Examples: CH4 (tetrahedral), NH3 (pyramidal, 107°), H2O (angular, ~104°).
  4. sp2 — one s + two p → three coplanar orbitals, 120°, 33.33% s, one p left over. Examples: BF3, C2H4 (5σ + 1π).
  5. sp — one s + one p → two co-linear orbitals, 180°, 50% s, two p left over. Examples: BeCl2, C2H2 (3σ + 2π, a triple bond).
  6. Hybrid orbitals make sigma bonds only; leftover unhybridized p orbitals make the pi bonds. More s-character = stronger bond.
