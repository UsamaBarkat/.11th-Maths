2️⃣ Short Questions

1. Define the following: (i) Bond Energy  (ii) Bond length

(i) Bond Energy: "The energy involved for the breaking / formation of 1 mole of a particular type of bonds in the molecule is known as bond energy." Since measurement in bond dissociation is more accurate than in bond formation, it is generally taken as the bond dissociation energy and written with a positive sign. Unit: kJ/mol.

(ii) Bond length: "The distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule is known as bond length." Because bonded atoms are always in vibrational motion, an average value is taken. It is determined by the x-ray diffraction technique and measured in Angstrom (Å) = 10−10 m.

2. The dipole moment of water is 1.85 D but CO2 has zero dipole moment. Why?

Answer: Because the dipole moment of a molecule depends not only upon the polarity of its bonds but also upon the geometry of the molecule, and dipole moment is a vector quantity.
  • H2O has an angular structure (104.5°). The two polar O–H bond dipoles are not opposite, so they do not cancel — they combine to give a net dipole moment of 1.85 D. Water is polar.
  • CO2 is linear. Although both C=O bonds are polar, they are directed equally on opposite sides at a 180° angle, so the two dipoles are equal and opposite and cancel completely. Hence μ = 0 and the molecule is non-polar.

3. Differentiate between the following: (i) VBT and MOT (ii) Sigma bond and pi bond (iii) Bonding M.O and Anti bonding M.O

(i) VBT vs MOT
  • VBT: only some valence electrons are involved in bond formation. MOT: all the valence electrons of interacting atoms are involved.
  • VBT: both atomic orbitals possess their individual identity. MOT: they do not possess their individual identity.
  • VBT gives no adequate idea of bond order. MOT gives an adequate idea, identifying the bond as single, double or triple.
  • VBT cannot explain the paramagnetic behaviour of O2. MOT explains it very well.

(ii) Sigma bond vs pi bond
  • Sigma is formed by head to head overlapping; pi by lateral/parallel overlapping of half filled atomic orbitals.
  • Only one sigma bond can exist between bonded atoms; a maximum of two pi bonds can form.
  • Free rotation is possible with only a sigma bond; it is not possible when a pi bond is present.
  • Sigma: electron cloud is denser at the plane of the bond axis. Pi: denser above and below that plane.

(iii) Bonding M.O vs Anti bonding M.O
  • Bonding MO has lower energy than the atomic orbitals; anti bonding MO has higher energy.
  • Bonding MO is produced by constructive interaction; anti bonding by destructive interaction.
  • In a bonding MO the electron density is maximum between the two nuclei; in an anti bonding MO it is zero, since a nodal plane bisects the inter-nucleus axis.
  • Electrons in a bonding MO favour bonding; those in an anti bonding MO oppose it.

4. Explain the following with scientific reasons: (i) Sigma bond is stronger than pi bond (ii) HF has greater ionic character than HCl (iii) Bond energy of molecules possessing multiple bonds is high

(i) Sigma bond is stronger than pi bond
Bond strength is proportional to the extent of overlapping. A sigma bond forms by head to head overlapping, so the orbitals meet along the line joining the nuclei and the overlap is maximum, with the electron cloud densest between the two nuclei. A pi bond forms by lateral overlapping of parallel p-orbitals, where the region of overlap is small (minimum) and the cloud is split above and below the axis. Greater overlap ⟹ stronger bond, so sigma > pi.

(ii) HF has greater ionic character than HCl
Ionic character depends mainly on the electronegativity difference between the bonded atoms. For H–F the difference is 1.9 (F = 4.0, H = 2.1), giving 43% ionic character. For H–Cl it is only 0.9 (Cl = 3.0), giving 17%. Since fluorine is far more electronegative, it pulls the shared electron pair much more strongly, producing larger partial charges and hence greater ionic character.

(iii) Bond energy of molecules possessing multiple bonds is high
A multiple bond means more bonds holding the same two atoms together — a double bond is 1σ + 1π and a triple bond is 1σ + 2π. Breaking the molecule means breaking all of them, so more energy is required: C–C 348 < C=C 614 < C≡C 839 kJ/mol. Note, however, that the rise is not proportional, because the pi bond is weaker than the sigma bond and needs less energy to break.

5. Oil is insoluble in water but soluble in hexane. Explain why.

Answer: The criterion for solubility is whether the substances are polar or non-polar — like dissolves like.
  • Oil in water — insoluble. Oil is a non-polar organic liquid whereas water is polar. The positive and negative poles of water behave hydrophobically towards oil and do not allow it to dissolve, so the two form separate layers.
  • Oil in hexane — soluble. Both oil and hexane are non-polar, held by weak London forces. Since the interactions among the particles of both are weak and comparable, on dissolution the attractive forces of the solvent are enough to overcome the attractive forces among oil molecules, hence it dissolves.

6. Which hybridization would you expect for the central atom in each of the following? BeCl2, BF3, CH4, C2H2

Answer:
  • BeCl2 → sp. Excited beryllium (1s2, 2s1, 2px1) mixes 2s and 2px to give two sp hybrid orbitals at 180° — linear geometry.
  • BF3 → sp2. Excited boron mixes one s and two p orbitals to give three sp2 hybrid orbitals, coplanar at 120° — planar triangular geometry.
  • CH4 → sp3. Excited carbon mixes one s and three p orbitals to give four sp3 hybrid orbitals pointing at the corners of a tetrahedron at 109.5°.
  • C2H2 → sp. Each carbon mixes only s and px to give two sp hybrid orbitals at 180° (linear); py and pz stay unhybridized and form two pi bonds.

3️⃣ Descriptive Questions

1. (a) Write down the postulates of VSEPR theory. (b) Predict the shape of the following molecules on the basis of VSEPR theory: AlCl3, CCl4, PH3, H2S

(a) Postulates of VSEPR theory (Sidgwick and Powell, 1940):
  1. There may be two types of electron pairs surrounding the central atom. Bond pairs result from the sharing of unpaired electrons of the central atom with unpaired electrons of surrounding atoms. Lone pairs are the paired electrons of the central atom which do not take part in sharing (non-bonding pairs). Both are considered an active set of electrons.
  2. Being similarly charged (negative), the bond pairs as well as the lone pairs repel each other.
  3. Due to repulsion, the electron pairs try to be as far apart as possible, orienting themselves so that the force of repulsion is minimized.
  4. The force of repulsion is not the same for all pairs. The order is Lone pair–Lone pair > Lone pair–Bond pair > Bond pair–Bond pair.
  5. In molecules with double and triple bonds, the π electron pairs are not an active set of electrons and are not included in the count of total electron pairs.
  6. The shape of a molecule depends upon the total number of electron pairs (bonding and lone pairs).

2. What do you mean by Hybridization? Explain sp3 hybridization in CH4 molecule and sp2 hybridization in C2H4 molecule.

Hybridization: "The mixing of different atomic orbitals to produce the same number of equivalent orbitals, having the same shape and energy, is known as hybridization." The orbitals so formed are called hybrid orbitals and are designated according to the number of mixing orbitals. The idea was suggested by Linus Pauling, who noted that atomic orbitals of nearly equal energies can be mixed within an atom.

sp3 hybridization in CH4
The configuration of carbon is 1s2, 2s2, 2px1, 2py1, 2pz0. One electron of the 2s orbital is promoted to the 2pz orbital to make carbon tetravalent: 1s2, 2s1, 2px1, 2py1, 2pz1.
These four atomic orbitals undergo mixing to produce four new orbitals of equal energy and shape, each an sp3 hybrid orbital. All four are directed towards the corners of a regular tetrahedron at 109.5°. Since each sp3 hybrid orbital has one electron, it overlaps with the 1s orbital of a hydrogen atom, forming four C–H sigma bonds. Each hybrid has 25% s and 75% p character.

sp2 hybridization in C2H4
Ethene consists of two central carbon atoms. Each carbon possesses three sp2 hybrid orbitals (one s + two p, 33.33% s character) and one unhybrid pz orbital.
  • The three sp2 hybrid orbitals arrange in triangular planar geometry at 120°.
  • Two sp2 orbitals of each carbon overlap with the s orbitals of two hydrogen atoms → two C–H sigma bonds per carbon.
  • The third sp2 orbital of both carbons overlaps linearly with each other → C–C sigma bond.
  • The unhybrid pz orbitals of both carbons, lying at right angles, overlap side-wise → one pi bond.
Total: 5 sigma bonds and 1 pi bond, making the C=C double bond.

3. What are the basic postulates of valence bond theory? Explain the various types of overlapping that lead to sigma and pi bonds.

Postulates of VBT (London and Heitler, 1927):
  1. A covalent bond is formed due to the overlap of half filled orbitals of combining atoms (overlap = sharing of some common region in space).
  2. As a result of overlapping there is a maximum electron cloud somewhere between the two overlapped orbitals.
  3. The electrons present in both overlapped orbitals should be in opposite spin.
  4. The strength of the bond is determined by the extent of overlap — the greater the overlap, the stronger the bond.
  5. Each atom involved in the overlapping keeps its own atomic orbital, but the electron pair is shared by both atoms.

Types of overlapping → SIGMA bond ("the bond formed by head to head overlapping of half filled atomic orbitals"; electron density highest between the two bonded nuclei):
  • s–s overlapping — overlap of the half filled s orbital of two combining atoms. Example: H2. Each hydrogen has one electron in 1s; the two half filled s-orbitals overlap to form the H–H sigma bond.
  • s–p overlapping — overlap of a half filled s and a half filled p orbital. Examples: HF, HCl. In HF the 1s orbital of hydrogen overlaps with the half filled 2pz orbital of fluorine.
  • p–p overlapping — overlap of the half filled p orbitals of two atoms. Example: F2. Fluorine is 1s2 2s2 2px2 2py2 2pz1, so only the pz orbital is half filled and available for overlap.
Overlapping → PI bond
Formed by the lateral or side-way overlap of p-orbitals between two atoms which are already bonded through a sigma bond. The two p-orbitals must be parallel. The electron cloud is unsymmetrical, concentrated above and below the plane of the sigma bond, and rotation becomes restricted. Because the region of overlap is small, a pi bond is weaker than a sigma bond. Examples: O2, N2 — in O2 each atom has two half filled p orbitals, one overlapping head to head (sigma) and the other laterally (pi).

Order of strength: s–s < s–p < p–p for sigma bonds (p-orbitals have directional charge distribution and longer lobes, giving more effective overlap), and pi < sigma overall.

4. Draw a molecular orbital diagram of O2 molecule and (i) write down MO electronic configuration (ii) determine bond order (iii) explain the paramagnetic behaviour.

(i) MO electronic configuration
The molecular orbital configuration with increasing energy order is given as:
σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < π2py = π2pz < σ2px < π*2py = π*2pz < σ*2px
Filled for O2's 16 electrons:
σ1s2 σ*1s2 σ2s2 σ*2s2 σ2px2 π2py2 π2pz2 π*2py1 π*2pz1
(ii) Bond order
N(b) = σ1s² + σ2s² + σ2px² + π2py² + π2pz² = 10
N(a) = σ*1s² + σ*2s² + π*2py¹ + π*2pz¹ = 6
Bond order = N(b) − N(a)2 = 10 − 62 = 2
A bond order of 2 confirms the double bond in O=O.

(iii) Paramagnetic behaviour
The last two electrons enter the two degenerate anti bonding orbitals π*2py and π*2pz. By Hund's rule they occupy them singly with parallel spin rather than pairing up. Therefore O2 has two unpaired electrons in the anti bonding MO.
Substances with unpaired electrons are attracted by an external magnetic field and are said to be paramagnetic — hence O2 is paramagnetic. This is the key success of MOT: VBT predicts all electrons paired and therefore fails to explain oxygen's magnetism.

📝 Chapter Summary with Key Terms

The whole chapter in ten lines

  • Valence Bond Theory tells that a covalent bond is formed by the overlapping of atomic orbitals, in which the shared pair of electrons is localized between the two nuclei.
  • Molecular Orbital Theory tells that when two atomic orbitals combine they form two molecular orbitals — one of lower energy (bonding M.O) and one of higher energy than the atomic orbital (anti bonding M.O).
  • Sigma bond is formed by head to head overlapping of atomic orbitals. It exists in s–s, s–p, p–p etc.
  • Pi bond is formed by the side-way overlapping of atomic orbitals (py–py and pz–pz). It is relatively weaker than a sigma bond.
  • Hybridization is the mixing of atomic orbitals to produce an equal number of same-energy orbitals. It is of various types such as sp3, sp2, sp, depending on the number and type of orbitals involved.
  • Electron pair repulsion theory describes the shape of simple covalent molecules on the basis of the number of active electron pairs around the central atom.
  • Dipole moment is the product of charge and the distance between positive and negative centres. Measured in Debye; S.I unit coulomb metre.
  • Bond length is the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule. Measured in Angstrom (Å).
  • Bond energy is the amount of energy required to break 1 mole of a particular type of bond in the molecule. Measured in kJ/mol.
  • Ionic character of a covalent bond is the polarity of the molecule produced when the electrons of two unlike atoms are shared up.
