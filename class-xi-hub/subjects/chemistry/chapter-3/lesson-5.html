Bond Energy & Bond Length

Chapter 3 · Lesson 5 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 13–15 min read Topics 3.3.1 & 3.3.2

🎯 Why Should You Care?

So far you have described bonds with words — strong, weak, single, double. This lesson turns those words into numbers you can compare.

Two numbers do the job. Bond energy tells you how much energy it takes to break a bond. Bond length tells you how far apart the two nuclei sit. And they are linked by one simple rule: shorter bonds are stronger bonds.

Once you can look up these numbers you can predict which reaction will happen, which molecule is more stable, and why a nitrogen molecule is so unreactive that it makes up most of the air you are breathing right now.

📖 Bond Characteristics

There are various characteristics of a chemical bond such as bond length, bond energy, bond order and dipole moment etc. All of these describe the strength and stability of a bond.

This lesson covers bond energy and bond length. Ionic character and dipole moment follow in Lesson 6.

⚡ Bond Energy

The formation and decomposition of a chemical bond are two opposite processes, both associated with an energy change:

  • Bond formation is an exothermic process (energy released).
  • Bond breaking is an endothermic process (energy absorbed).
📚 Definition

"The energy involved for the breaking / formation of 1 mole of a particular type of bonds in the molecule is known as bond energy."

Alternatively, the bond energy might be taken as the energy released in the formation of a bond from its free atoms. It is important to note that the measurement of energy in bond dissociation is more accurate than bond formation, thus bond energy is generally considered as bond dissociation energy and written in terms of a positive sign.

Since chemical reactions take place at constant pressure, the term enthalpy is often used. It is the energy required in kJ for the breaking of one mole of bonds in the given sample of reactant.

O2(g)→O(g) + O(g)  (ΔH = +495 kJ/mol)
O(g) + O(g)→O2(g)  (ΔH = −495 kJ/mol)
±
Same number, opposite sign

Breaking the O=O bond costs +495 kJ/mol; forming it releases −495 kJ/mol. The magnitude is identical — only the sign flips. By convention we quote bond energy as the positive (dissociation) value.

💪 Comparison of Bond Energy with Bond Strength

Bond energy is a very useful tool in determining the strength of a bond, and hence helps in understanding chemical reactivity. Bond energy and the strength of a bond can be predicted on the basis of three parameters.

(i) Polarity of the molecule

The value of bond energy in a diatomic molecule depends upon whether the molecule is homo-diatomic or hetero-diatomic. The bond energy of a hetero-diatomic molecule is relatively higher, since the atoms lie closer due to the presence of ionic character on the bonded atoms.

HCl(g)→H(g) + Cl(g)  (ΔH = 431 kJ/mol)
Cl2(g)→Cl(g) + Cl(g)  (ΔH = 242 kJ/mol)

In the above atomization processes, we note that the bond energy of HCl is higher due to polarity in the molecule.

(ii) Size of the bonded atoms

The value of bond energy can be correlated with the size of the atoms. Two atoms of smaller size are bonded more strongly due to the short inter-nuclear distance, thus they can be broken only if high energy is provided.

H2(g)→H(g) + H(g)  (ΔH = 436 kJ/mol)
Cl2(g)→Cl(g) + Cl(g)  (ΔH = 242 kJ/mol)

The high bond energy is due to the smaller size of hydrogen atoms. Thus not only the polarity but also the size of atoms in the molecule are involved in bond energy.

(iii) Presence of multiple bonds

Generally molecules with multiple bonds require high bond energy for dissociation. Consider the bond energies involved in carbon–carbon bond breaking:

C – C (348 kJ/mol)  <  C = C (614 kJ/mol)  <  C ≡ C (839 kJ/mol)

This shows that the greater the number of bonds between two atoms, the higher the bond energy.

🔍
Why a double bond isn't exactly twice as strong

It may be noted that the bond energy of C=C is not exactly double that of C–C, and similarly C≡C is not exactly three times higher. The reason is that a double bond between two atoms is one sigma and one pi bond. Since the pi bond is relatively weak and requires less energy for breaking, the total falls short of a simple multiple. The same reason applies to C≡C.

Self Assessment (i) What are the various parameters which correlate bond energy with bond strength?
Answer — three parameters
  1. Polarity of the molecule — hetero-diatomic molecules have higher bond energy than homo-diatomic ones, because ionic character brings the atoms closer (HCl 431 > Cl2 242 kJ/mol).
  2. Size of the bonded atoms — smaller atoms bond more strongly due to a short inter-nuclear distance (H2 436 > Cl2 242 kJ/mol).
  3. Presence of multiple bonds — more bonds between two atoms means higher bond energy (C–C 348 < C=C 614 < C≡C 839 kJ/mol).
Self Assessment (ii) Why is the bond energy of a multiple bond greater than a single bond?
Answer

Because a multiple bond contains more than one bond holding the two atoms together. A single bond is one sigma bond; a double bond is one sigma + one pi; a triple bond is one sigma + two pi. Breaking a multiple bond means breaking all of them, so more energy is required.

However, the increase is not proportional — C=C (614) is less than double C–C (348) — because the pi bond is weaker than the sigma bond and needs less energy to break.

📏 Bond Length

A molecule is the combination of atoms formed by mutual sharing of electrons. Atoms in a molecule are located at a certain distance to balance the attractive and repulsive forces between nucleus and electrons.

📚 Definition

"The distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule is known as bond length."

  • Since bonded atoms are always in vibrational motion due to their kinetic energy, a precise bond length cannot be determined — hence we take the average of the bond length.
  • Bond length is experimentally determined by the x-ray diffraction technique.
  • Bond length is a very small distance and the unit used for its measurement is the Angstrom (Å), which is equal to 10−10 m.
The shorter the bond length, the stronger is the bond.

We can mainly correlate the bond length with the following two parameters.

(i) Bond order

The greater the number of bonds between the two atoms, the shorter the bond length. For instance, the bond length in C≡C is shortest, then C=C, then C–C.

(ii) Bond polarity

The bond length between two different atoms is generally higher than between similar atoms, and its value depends upon the extent of polarity. For example, the bond length of H–Cl (1.27 Å) is shorter than Cl–Cl (1.99 Å).

💡
Do You Know? — covalent radius

A covalent radius is half of the bond length between the nuclei of two identical atoms which are bonded through a single covalent bond.

Bond energy, bond length and bond order are the three essential bond parameters which describe the strength of a covalent bond. Bond order came from MOT in Lesson 2 — this is where it pays off.

💡 Real-Life Way to Think About It

  1. One rope vs three ropes 🪢 — A triple bond is like tying two poles with three ropes instead of one. Harder to cut (higher energy) and it pulls the poles closer together (shorter length).
  2. Magnets of different sizes 🧲 — Two small magnets stick together far more firmly than two big ones, because they can get closer. That is why H2 (436) beats Cl2 (242).
  3. Nitrogen in the air 🌬️ — N≡N at 941 kJ/mol is so hard to break that nitrogen gas barely reacts with anything. That single number explains why 78% of the atmosphere just sits there harmlessly.
  4. A vibrating guitar string 🎸 — Bonded atoms never stop vibrating, so the "length" is really an average position — which is exactly what the textbook says.

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "A double bond has exactly twice the energy of a single bond."

❌ C–C = 348 but C=C = 614, not 696. A double bond is one sigma + one weaker pi, so the total is always less than a simple multiple.

⚠️ "Longer bonds are stronger because there's more of them."

❌ The opposite. Shorter bond = stronger bond, because the nuclei are closer and attract the shared electrons more tightly. Compare C≡C (1.20 Å, 839) with C–C (1.54 Å, 348).

⚠️ "Bond formation absorbs energy."

❌ Bond formation is exothermic (releases energy); bond breaking is endothermic (absorbs energy). Students flip these constantly — the O2 example with ±495 kJ/mol is the one to memorise.

⚠️ "Homo-diatomic molecules have higher bond energy — they're the same atom, so a better fit."

❌ Hetero-diatomic molecules have the higher bond energy, because ionic character pulls the atoms closer. HCl (431) > Cl2 (242).

⚠️ "Bond length is measured directly with an instrument."

⚠️ It is determined by the x-ray diffraction technique, and because atoms are always vibrating, the value quoted is an average, not an exact fixed distance.

Short Questions

Q6. Define bond energy and bond length.

Bond energy: "The energy involved for the breaking / formation of 1 mole of a particular type of bonds in the molecule." It is measured in kJ/mol and is generally quoted as the positive bond dissociation energy.

Bond length: "The distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule." It is measured in Angstrom (Å) = 10−10 m and determined by x-ray diffraction.

Q7. Why is the bond energy of HCl higher than that of Cl2?

Answer: Because HCl is a hetero-diatomic (polar) molecule while Cl2 is homo-diatomic (non-polar). The presence of ionic character in HCl makes the atoms lie closer together, which strengthens the bond. HCl = 431 kJ/mol vs Cl2 = 242 kJ/mol.

Q8. Why is H2 (436 kJ/mol) stronger than Cl2 (242 kJ/mol) even though both are non-polar?

Answer: Because of the size of the bonded atoms. Hydrogen atoms are much smaller than chlorine atoms, so the inter-nuclear distance is short and the two atoms are bonded more strongly. Higher energy is therefore needed to break the H–H bond.

Q9. Arrange C–C, C=C and C≡C in order of increasing bond length and explain the trend.

Answer: C≡C (1.20 Å) < C=C (1.34 Å) < C–C (1.54 Å). As the bond order increases, more bonds pull the two nuclei closer together, so the bond length decreases. Correspondingly the bond energy rises: 348 → 614 → 839 kJ/mol. Shorter bond = stronger bond.

📝 Summary

6 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. Bond energy = energy for breaking/forming 1 mole of a particular bond, in kJ/mol. Formation is exothermic, breaking is endothermic; the value is quoted positive as the dissociation energy.
  2. Three parameters raise bond energy: polarity (HCl 431 > Cl2 242), smaller atoms (H2 436 > Cl2 242), and multiple bonds (C–C 348 < C=C 614 < C≡C 839).
  3. A double bond is not exactly twice a single bond, because the pi bond is weaker than the sigma bond.
  4. Bond length = distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms, measured in Å (10−10 m) by x-ray diffraction; because atoms vibrate, it is an average.
  5. Bond length depends on bond order (more bonds → shorter) and bond polarity (H–Cl 1.27 Å < Cl–Cl 1.99 Å). A covalent radius is half the bond length between two identical bonded atoms.
  6. Bond energy, bond length and bond order are the three essential parameters describing bond strength — and they always move together: shorter = stronger = higher order.
