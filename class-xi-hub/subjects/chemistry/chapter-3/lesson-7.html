Effect of Bonding on Physical & Chemical Properties

Chapter 3 · Lesson 7 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 10–12 min read Topic 3.4

🎯 Why Should You Care?

Spill cooking oil into water and it just floats there in a separate layer, refusing to mix. Spill it into petrol and it vanishes instantly. Same oil. Completely different outcome.

Everything you have learned in this chapter — overlap, shape, polarity, dipole moment — has been building to this. Now you can predict that behaviour instead of just observing it.

This lesson closes the loop: the nature and strength of a bond decides the real, visible properties of a substance — whether it dissolves, how it melts, how fast it reacts.

📖 What Bonding Controls

The nature and strength of a bond affects various physical and chemical properties of a substance, such as solubility, melting point, boiling point, rate of reaction etc.

💧 Solubility of Polar and Non-Polar Covalent Compounds

It is well established that certain substances dissolve in a liquid solvent while others do not. The criterion for deciding solubility is to know whether they are polar or non-polar. Many organic compounds are non-polar in nature and cannot be dissolved in water, however they are soluble in organic solvents.

The solubility of a covalent compound depends upon the chemical nature of the solvent and the substance to be dissolved.

🔑
The rule the whole topic hangs on

Like dissolves like. Polar dissolves polar. Non-polar dissolves non-polar. Mixing the two types does not work. All three cases below are just this rule applied three different ways.

(i) Solubility of a polar substance in a polar solvent ✅

A polar covalent molecule is as a whole neutral, but it possesses partially positive and negative charge on its two ends. Thus when it is added into a polar solvent, a dipole–dipole interaction is developed among the solute, solvent and particles, which makes it soluble.

Example — sugar in water. We know that sugar is easily dissolved in water. This is due to the fact that several O–H bonds in the sugar molecule bear partially positive and negative charge on them. These polar areas of the sugar molecule interact with the positive and negative poles of water molecules to form hydrogen bonds. Due to this interaction, sugar is soluble in water.

(ii) Solubility of a non-polar substance in a polar solvent ❌

Non-polar covalent molecules do not dissolve in a polar solvent.

Example — benzene in water. When benzene is put into water, it does not dissolve and forms two layers. The reason is that benzene is non-polar whereas water is polar in nature. Thus the positive and negative poles of water behave hydrophobically with benzene and do not allow it to dissolve.

(iii) Solubility of a non-polar substance in a non-polar solvent ✅

Non-polar covalent molecules dissolve in a non-polar solvent on the basic principle that like dissolves like, due to London forces.

Example — oil in hexane. Oil is dissolved in hexane because both are non-polar. Since the interaction among the particles of both oil and hexane are weak and comparable, on dissolution the attractive forces of the solvent are enough to overcome the attractive forces among oil molecules, hence it dissolves.

   SOLUTE          SOLVENT         RESULT      WHY

   polar     +     polar      →    ✅ soluble   dipole–dipole
   (sugar)         (water)                     interaction, H-bonds

   non-polar +     polar      →    ❌ two       water behaves
   (benzene)       (water)          layers      hydrophobically

   non-polar +     non-polar  →    ✅ soluble   London forces,
   (oil)           (hexane)                     like dissolves like
Three combinations, one rule

Activity from the book — try it yourself

This activity will help you understand the solubility of a liquid in another liquid. Take two test tubes and put a few drops of cooking oil in both of them. Now add water to the first test tube and petrol to the second one, and shake both vigorously.

You will find that oil doesn't dissolve in water but in petrol it does. The reason is that oil is a non-polar organic liquid; it doesn't dissolve in water since water is polar in nature, while it dissolves in petrol due to petrol's non-polar nature.

⚗️ Reactions of Polar and Non-Polar Covalent Compounds

Chemical reactions take place by the breaking of existing bonds and the formation of some new bonds. Breaking of a bond requires energy.

The stronger the bond in the reactant molecule, the higher the energy needed for their breaking. Since the atoms in a polar covalent molecule are held more firmly, a high bond dissociation energy is required to proceed with the reaction.

📚 How reaction feasibility is judged

The feasibility of a chemical reaction is determined by having the information of the bond energy required to break the total number of reactant molecules and the bond energy released in bond formation in the total number of product molecules.

   REACTANTS  ──── break bonds ────→  atoms  ──── form bonds ────→  PRODUCTS
                  (costs energy)                (releases energy)
                   ENDOTHERMIC                    EXOTHERMIC

   Compare the two totals:
     energy released  >  energy required   →  reaction is favourable
     energy released  <  energy required   →  reaction is unfavourable
Every reaction is a balance sheet of bond energies

🔬 Society, Technology and Science

💇
Disulphide bond and hydrogen bond in straight and curly hair

Hair is composed of a type of protein known as keratin. In the structure of keratin, many amino acids (cystein) link to each other by permanent disulphide bonds. The distance between the sulphur atoms causes hair to curl — the greater the distance among sulphur atoms, the more the keratin molecule bends and the more our hair curls.

Another type of connection among neighbouring amino acid chains is the hydrogen bond, which is much weaker and temporary. This hydrogen bond is affected by water. On a humid day, moisture in the atmosphere breaks the hydrogen bonds of hair and ruins the hairstyle.

💡
Why this example is worth remembering

It shows both kinds of bond strength in one place. The strong, permanent disulphide bonds set whether your hair is straight or curly for life. The weak, temporary hydrogen bonds are what a hairstyle relies on — and why humidity destroys it in minutes.

💡 Real-Life Way to Think About It

  1. Washing greasy dishes 🍽️ — Water alone cannot shift grease, because grease is non-polar and water is polar. Detergent works because it has a polar end and a non-polar end, so it can hold on to both.
  2. Oil floating on a puddle 🌈 — Those rainbow films on wet roads are non-polar oil refusing to dissolve in polar water, exactly like the benzene example.
  3. Sugar in tea vs sugar in oil 🍵 — Sugar disappears into tea (polar + polar, hydrogen bonds form) but sits at the bottom of a bowl of oil forever.
  4. A frizzy day 💨 — When your hair misbehaves in humid weather, that is atmospheric moisture breaking the weak hydrogen bonds in your keratin. Real chemistry, visible in the mirror.

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "Water dissolves everything — it's the universal solvent."

❌ Water dissolves polar substances well. It fails completely on non-polar ones like benzene and oil, which form two layers instead. Many organic compounds are non-polar and are insoluble in water.

⚠️ "Non-polar molecules have no forces between them at all."

❌ They are held by London forces — weak, but real. It is precisely because these forces are weak and comparable in both solute and solvent that a non-polar solute dissolves in a non-polar solvent.

⚠️ "Stronger bonds make a substance react faster."

❌ The opposite. Stronger bonds need higher energy to break, so the reaction is harder to get going. Polar covalent molecules hold their atoms more firmly and need a high bond dissociation energy.

⚠️ "Sugar dissolves in water because it breaks into ions."

❌ Sugar is a covalent compound and does not ionise. It dissolves because its many polar O–H bonds form hydrogen bonds with the poles of water molecules.

⚠️ "Curly hair is caused by hydrogen bonds."

❌ Curl is set by the permanent disulphide bonds between cystein units and the distance between the sulphur atoms. Hydrogen bonds are weak and temporary — they only control your hairstyle, which is why humidity ruins it.

Short Questions

Q5. Oil is insoluble in water but soluble in hexane. Explain why.

Answer: Oil is a non-polar organic liquid. Water is polar, so its positive and negative poles behave hydrophobically towards oil and do not allow it to dissolve — the two form separate layers. Hexane is non-polar, so on the principle that like dissolves like, the weak London forces between hexane molecules are comparable to those between oil molecules. The solvent's attractive forces are enough to overcome the attractive forces among oil molecules, so oil dissolves.

Q6. How is the feasibility of a chemical reaction determined from bond energies?

Answer: A reaction proceeds by breaking existing bonds (which requires energy) and forming new bonds (which releases energy). The feasibility is determined by comparing the bond energy required to break the total number of reactant molecules with the bond energy released in forming the total number of product molecules.

Q7. Why do polar covalent compounds need more energy to react?

Answer: Because the atoms in a polar covalent molecule are held more firmly — the ionic character brings the atoms closer and strengthens the bond. The stronger the bond in the reactant molecule, the higher the energy needed for breaking it, so a high bond dissociation energy is required for the reaction to proceed.

Q8. Why does humidity ruin a hairstyle?

Answer: Neighbouring amino acid chains in keratin are connected by hydrogen bonds, which are much weaker and temporary compared with the permanent disulphide bonds. On a humid day, the moisture of the atmosphere breaks these hydrogen bonds, so the hair loses its set shape.

📝 Summary

5 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. The nature and strength of a bond affects solubility, melting point, boiling point and rate of reaction.
  2. Polar in polar → soluble, through dipole–dipole interaction (sugar in water, via hydrogen bonds with its O–H groups).
  3. Non-polar in polar → insoluble (benzene in water forms two layers; water behaves hydrophobically).
  4. Non-polar in non-polar → soluble, through London forces — like dissolves like (oil in hexane, oil in petrol).
  5. Reactions break bonds (needs energy) and form bonds (releases energy). Polar covalent molecules hold atoms more firmly, so they need a high bond dissociation energy; feasibility is judged by comparing total energy required against total energy released.
