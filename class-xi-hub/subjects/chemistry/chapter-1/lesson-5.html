Exponential (Scientific) Notation

Chapter 1 · Lesson 5 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 12–14 min read

🎯 Why Should You Care?

The mass of one electron is 0.000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 911 g. Imagine writing that in every calculation — you would lose count of the zeros and make mistakes constantly.

Scientists fix this with exponential notation: that same mass becomes a tidy 9.11 × 10−28 g. It is the standard language for the very large and the very small — which is exactly what chemistry deals with.

📖 Core Concept: N × 10x

📚 Definition

Exponential notation expresses a number as the product of two numbers: N × 10x, where N (the coefficient) is between 1 and 9.999, 10 is the base, and x (the exponent) is a positive or negative integer.

N × 10x     (coefficient × baseexponent)

Converting Standard ↔ Exponential

DirectionRuleExample
Standard → Exponential (big number)Move decimal left; exponent is positive4 600 000 = 4.6 × 106
Standard → Exponential (small number)Move decimal right; exponent is negative0.00038 = 3.8 × 10−4
Exponential → Standard (positive x)Move decimal right7.53 × 104 = 75 300
Exponential → Standard (negative x)Move decimal left4.87 × 10−3 = 0.00487

💡 Doing Maths with Exponentials

(i) Addition & Subtraction

First make the exponents the same, then add or subtract the coefficients.

Example 1.10 Add 1.31 × 103 and 3.15 × 102.

Convert 3.15 × 102 to the same exponent: 3.15 × 102 = 0.315 × 103. Now add coefficients:

1.31 × 103 + 0.315 × 103
Answer= 1.625 × 103

(ii) Multiplication

Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents.

Example 1.11 Multiply 7.0 × 1012 and 2.0 × 10−3.
(7.0 × 2.0) × 1012 + (−3) = 14 × 109
Answer= 1.4 × 1010

(iii) Division

Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

Example 1.12 Divide 6.60 × 108 by 3.20 × 103.
6.603.20 × 108 − 3
Answer= 2.06 × 105

(iv) Powers

Raise the coefficient to the power and multiply the exponent by that power.

Example 1.13 Simplify (3.25 × 104)2.
(3.25)2 × 104 × 2 = 10.56 × 108
Answer= 1.056 × 109

(v) Roots

Adjust the exponent so it divides evenly by the root, then take the root of the coefficient and divide the exponent.

Example 1.14 Simplify √(2.5 × 109).

Adjust 2.5 × 109 to 25 × 108 (so the exponent is even):

√(25 × 108) = √25 × 108/2 = 5 × 104
Answer= 5.0 × 104

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "When multiplying, multiply the exponents too."

❌ For multiplication you add the exponents (and multiply the coefficients). You only multiply the exponent when raising to a power.

⚠️ "Adding exponentials is like multiplying them."

❌ To add or subtract, the exponents must match first, then you combine the coefficients. You never add the exponents when adding numbers.

⚠️ "The coefficient can be any number."

✅ In proper scientific notation the coefficient N is between 1 and 9.999. That is why 14 × 109 is rewritten as 1.4 × 1010.

📝 Summary

3 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. Scientific notation is N × 10x, with the coefficient N between 1 and 9.999.
  2. Multiply → add exponents; divide → subtract exponents; power → multiply the exponent.
  3. To add/subtract, first make the exponents equal, then combine the coefficients.
