The Mole & Avogadro's Number

Chapter 1 · Lesson 1 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 10–12 min read

🎯 Why Should You Care?

When you buy bananas you ask for a dozen (12). Paper comes in a ream (500 sheets). Eggs come in a dozen too. We group things into fixed numbers so counting is easy.

Atoms are unbelievably tiny — a single drop of water holds more atoms than there are stars in the universe. We could never count them one by one. So chemists invented their own "dozen" for atoms. They call it the mole — and it lets us count atoms simply by weighing them.

📖 Core Concept: What Is a Mole?

📚 Textbook Definition

A mole is defined as the gram atomic mass, gram molecular mass, or gram formula mass of any substance (atoms, molecules, ions) which contains 6.02 × 1023 particles.

✅ Easy Version

A mole is just a counting word — like "dozen", but much bigger. One mole of anything means 6.02 × 1023 of those things. One mole of atoms = 6.02 × 1023 atoms. One mole of eggs would be 6.02 × 1023 eggs!

Avogadro's Number (NA)

That special number has a name. It is called Avogadro's number, after the Italian scientist Amedeo Avogadro.

📚 Definition

The number of particles present in one mole of any substance is called Avogadro's number. It is denoted by NA and its value is 6.02 × 1023.

💡
Do You Know?

6.02 × 1023 is such a huge value that the number of atoms in just one teaspoon of water, written out in full, would have more zeros than you could fit on this page.

Molar Mass

📚 Definition

The mass in grams of one mole of any pure substance is known as molar mass. Its unit is g/mol.

Here is the beautiful part: the molar mass is numerically equal to the atomic / molecular / formula mass. The atomic mass of carbon is 12, so the molar mass of carbon is 12 g/mol. The molecular mass of water (H2O) is 18, so its molar mass is 18 g/mol.

⚖️
The key idea

One mole of every substance contains the same number of particles (6.02 × 1023), but they weigh different amounts. 1 mole of carbon = 12 g, while 1 mole of magnesium = 24 g — twice as heavy, yet the same number of atoms.

Molar Volume

📚 Definition

The volume occupied by one mole of a gas at standard temperature (273 K, i.e. 0 °C) and pressure (1 atm) is called molar volume.

A remarkable fact: at STP, one mole of any ideal gas occupies the same volume:

Molar volume of any gas at STP = 22.4 dm3  (= 22.4 L)

So 1 mole of H2, 1 mole of O2 and 1 mole of CO2 each take up 22.4 dm3 at STP — even though they weigh 2 g, 32 g and 44 g. Molar volume can also be found from density:

Molar volume = Molar massDensity

💡 Real-Life Way to Think About It

  1. Counting by weighing 🌾 — A shopkeeper doesn't count 10,000 grains of rice; he weighs them. Chemists do the same with atoms using the mole.
  2. A dozen vs a mole 🍩 — "Dozen" = 12 things. "Mole" = 6.02 × 1023 things. Same idea, just a giant size.
  3. Same count, different weight ⚖️ — A dozen apples weighs more than a dozen grapes. Likewise a mole of lead weighs far more than a mole of helium — but both have 6.02 × 1023 particles.
  4. Gas balloons 🎈 — Fill three balloons with 1 mole each of H2, O2 and CO2 at STP; all three blow up to the same size, 22.4 dm3.

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "A mole is a kind of mass."

❌ No. A mole is a number (6.02 × 1023), just like "dozen" is a number. It is not grams. The molar mass is the mass of one mole — those are two different things.

⚠️ "Molar mass and molecular mass are the same."

✅ They are numerically equal but have different meanings. Molecular mass is a plain number (e.g. water = 18). Molar mass is that number with the unit g/mol (water = 18 g/mol). Molar mass is what we actually use in calculations.

⚠️ "Molar volume works for solids and liquids too."

❌ The 22.4 dm3 rule is only for gases at STP. Solids and liquids are far denser, so one mole of them takes up much less space.

Q4. Define a mole.

Answer: A mole is the gram atomic mass, gram molecular mass or gram formula mass of a substance that contains 6.02 × 1023 particles (atoms, molecules or ions).

Q5. Why do 1 mole of carbon (12 g) and 1 mole of magnesium (24 g) have the same number of atoms even though their masses differ?

Answer: Because a mole is defined by number, not by mass. Any mole contains 6.02 × 1023 particles. Magnesium atoms are simply heavier than carbon atoms, so the same count of them weighs twice as much.

📝 Summary

3 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. A mole is a counting unit meaning 6.02 × 1023 particles — this number is called Avogadro's number (NA).
  2. Molar mass is the mass of one mole in grams (g/mol); it is numerically equal to the atomic/molecular/formula mass.
  3. One mole of any gas at STP occupies a fixed molar volume of 22.4 dm3.
