① Multiple Choice Questions

(i) If the volume occupied by O2 at STP is 44.8 dm3, the number of molecules of O2 is:

44.8 ÷ 22.4 = 2 moles → 2 × 6.02 × 1023 = 12.04 × 1023 molecules.

(ii) The number of carbon atoms in 1 mole of sugar (C12H22O11) is approximately:

Each sugar molecule has 12 C atoms → 12 × 6.02 × 1023 ≈ 72 × 1023 atoms.

(iii) In 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2, if 23 g of Na reacts with excess water, the volume of H2 liberated at STP is:

23 g Na = 1 mole. 2 Na → 1 H2, so 1 mole Na → 0.5 mole H2 → 0.5 × 22.4 = 11.2 dm3.

(iv) Which sample contains the same number of atoms as 20 g of calcium (Ca = 40)?

20 g Ca ÷ 40 = 0.5 mole. We need another 0.5-mole sample: 16 g S ÷ 32 = 0.5 mole ✓ (same number of atoms).

(v) Which statement is incorrect?

1 mole of Cl2 = 2 × 35.5 = 71 g, not 35.5 g. So statement (a) is incorrect.

(vi) Which statement about Avogadro's number is incorrect?

Avogadro's number is a fixed constant (6.02 × 1023 per mole). It never changes with temperature or the number of moles — so any statement claiming it changes is wrong. The clearest such error here is (d).

(vii) The minimum number of moles is present in:

moles = V ÷ 22.4. Smallest volume → fewest moles. 1 ÷ 22.4 = 0.045 mole, the minimum.

(viii) The number of atoms in 60 g of carbon is:

60 ÷ 12 = 5 moles → 5 × 6.02 × 1023 = 30.1 × 1023 = 3.01 × 1024 atoms.

(ix) Maximum number of molecules is present in:

Most molecules = most moles = smallest molar mass. O2 (32) is the lightest, so 100 ÷ 32 = 3.13 moles gives the most molecules.

(x) Practical (actual) yield is:

Some product is always lost to side reactions and physical processes, so practical yield is less than theoretical yield.

② Short Questions

1. Define: (i) Stoichiometry  (ii) Exponential Notation  (iii) Molar Volume

(i) Stoichiometry: the study of the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a balanced chemical equation.

(ii) Exponential notation: a shorthand way of writing very large or very small numbers as N × 10x.

(iii) Molar volume: the volume occupied by one mole of a gas at STP; it equals 22.4 dm3 for any ideal gas.

2. Express in exponential notation: (i) 3652  (ii) 0.0231  (iii) 0.000072

(i) 3652 = 3.652 × 103  ·  (ii) 0.0231 = 2.31 × 10−2  ·  (iii) 0.000072 = 7.2 × 10−5

3. Express in simple numbers: (i) 3.26 × 103  (ii) 1.921 × 102  (iii) 1.02 × 104

(i) 3260  ·  (ii) 192.1  ·  (iii) 10200

4. Define rounding of data and give its rules.

Rounding of data means reducing a number to the desired significant figures and adjusting the last digit. Rules: (1) If the dropped digit is > 5, add 1 to the kept digit. (2) If it is < 5, leave the kept digit unchanged. (3) If it is exactly 5, make the kept digit even (leave it if already even, add 1 if odd).

③ Descriptive Questions

1. Define theoretical, actual and percent yield. Why is practical yield often less than theoretical yield?

Theoretical yield is the maximum product calculated from the balanced equation (using the limiting reactant). Actual (practical) yield is the amount really obtained in the experiment. Percent yield = (practical ÷ theoretical) × 100.

Practical yield is lower because some reactant may not react, side products may form, the reaction may be reversible, and product is lost during filtration, distillation, crystallisation or washing.

2. What is meant by mole and Avogadro's number? How are they related?

A mole is the amount of a substance that contains 6.02 × 1023 particles. Avogadro's number (NA = 6.02 × 1023) is the number of particles in one mole. They are directly related: one mole of any substance always contains exactly Avogadro's number of particles.

3. What is Avogadro's number? Explain the concept of the mole using it.

Avogadro's number is 6.02 × 1023, the count of particles in one mole. The mole uses this number as a "chemist's dozen": just as a dozen means 12, a mole means 6.02 × 1023. So one mole of atoms = 6.02 × 1023 atoms, letting chemists count tiny particles simply by weighing the molar mass.

4. What is a limiting reactant? How does it control the amount of product formed?

The limiting reactant is the reactant completely consumed first, while some of the other reactant is left over. Because the reaction stops the moment the limiting reactant runs out, the maximum product depends entirely on it — so all product calculations are based on the limiting reactant, not the excess one.
