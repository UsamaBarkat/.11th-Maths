Theoretical, Practical & Percent Yield

Chapter 1 · Lesson 7 of 7 🧪 Chemistry ⏱ 10–12 min read

🎯 Why Should You Care?

A recipe says 12 cupcakes, but a few stick to the tray, one burns, and you end up with 10. On paper you expected 12; in reality you got 10.

Chemistry is the same. The amount calculated on paper is the theoretical yield; the amount you actually collect is the practical yield. Comparing them — the percent yield — tells a chemist how efficient their reaction really was.

📖 Core Concept: Three Kinds of Yield

📚 Theoretical Yield

The maximum amount of product obtained from a balanced chemical reaction, calculated using the limiting reactant. It is the "best case" amount.

📚 Practical (Actual) Yield

The amount of product actually obtained in the experiment. It is usually less than the theoretical yield.

📚 Percent Yield

The ratio of practical yield to theoretical yield, expressed as a percentage:

Percent yield = Practical yieldTheoretical yield × 100

Why Is Practical Yield Less Than Theoretical?

  • Some of the reactant may not react at all.
  • Reactants may form unwanted side products.
  • The reaction may be reversible and not go to completion.
  • Product is lost during filtration, distillation, crystallisation or washing.
📉
Yield is never more than 100%

A real percent yield is always between 0% and 100%. If you ever calculate more than 100%, you have made an error (or the product still contains impurities).

💡 Worked Example

Example 1.17 50 g of CaCO3 reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. If 14.52 g of CO2 is liberated, calculate the theoretical yield and percent yield of CO2.
Balanced equation
CaCO3 + 2HCl→CaCl2 + CO2 + H2O
Step 1 — moles of CaCO3 (molar mass = 100)
Moles of CaCO3 = 50100 = 0.5 mole
Step 2 — theoretical yield of CO2

1 mole CaCO3 → 1 mole CO2, so 0.5 mole CaCO3 → 0.5 mole CO2.

Theoretical yield = 0.5 × 44 = 22 g
Step 3 — percent yield (practical yield = 14.52 g)
Percent yield = 14.5222 × 100
AnswerTheoretical yield = 22 g · Percent yield = 66%

⚠️ Common Confusions

⚠️ "Theoretical yield is what I get in the lab."

❌ Theoretical yield is the calculated maximum from the equation. What you actually collect is the practical yield, which is usually smaller.

⚠️ "Percent yield = theoretical ÷ practical × 100."

❌ It is the other way round: practical ÷ theoretical × 100. Practical (the real, smaller amount) goes on top.

⚠️ "Use the excess reactant to find theoretical yield."

✅ Theoretical yield is always based on the limiting reactant — the same idea from the previous lesson.

Q4. Give two reasons why practical yield is less than theoretical yield.

Answer: (1) Some reactant may not react or may form side products; (2) Product is lost during physical processes such as filtration, distillation, crystallisation or washing.

Q5. Under high pressure, 4 g of Mg reacts with excess O2 to produce 4.24 g of MgO. The theoretical yield is 6.66 g. Calculate the percent yield.

Answer: Percent yield = (4.24 ÷ 6.66) × 100 ≈ 63.7%.

📝 Summary

3 Key Points from This Lesson

  1. Theoretical yield = calculated maximum product (from the limiting reactant); practical yield = what you actually get.
  2. Percent yield = (practical ÷ theoretical) × 100 — practical goes on top.
  3. Practical yield is lower because of side reactions, reversibility, and losses during filtration/distillation/washing.
