What is Chemistry?

Chapter 1 · Lesson 1 · Estimated reading time: 10–15 min

🎯 Why Should You Care?

Right now, take a quick look around your room. You see: the air you breathe, a glass of water, your phone (glass + plastic), chai, salt, petrol in motorbikes outside.

Here's the magical truth: everything you just looked at is made of tiny invisible particles, and the study of these particles is called Chemistry. Even you are a walking chemistry experiment — your body is doing thousands of chemical reactions every second right now!

📖 Core Concept: What is Chemistry?

📚 Textbook Definition

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the composition, structure, properties, and changes of matter.

✅ Easy Version

Chemistry is the science of "what things are made of" and "how they change into other things."

What Do Those 4 Words Mean?

Word What it means Example
Composition What is it made of? Water = hydrogen + oxygen
Structure How are the pieces arranged? Diamond and pencil lead are both carbon, but arranged differently
Properties How does it behave? Salt dissolves in water; oil does not
Changes How does it turn into something else? Wood burning → ash + smoke

What is Matter?

📚 Definition

Matter = anything that has mass AND takes up space (volume).

        MATTER
          |
    ┌─────┴─────┐
    |           |
  Has mass    Takes space
  (weight)    (volume)

Is It Matter? — Quick Test

  • Air → has mass + takes space → ✅ Matter
  • A thought → no mass, no space → ❌ Not matter
  • Light → no mass → ❌ Not matter (it's energy)
  • School bag → has mass + takes space → ✅ Matter
  • Water → ✅ Matter

The 7 Branches of Chemistry

Branch What it studies Real-life example
Physical Chemistry Energy + speed of reactions Why ice melts faster in summer
Organic Chemistry Carbon-based compounds Petrol, plastic, sugar
Inorganic Chemistry Non-carbon compounds Salt, metals, water
Biochemistry Chemistry inside living bodies How your body digests biryani
Analytical Chemistry Finding what something is made of Lab blood tests
Nuclear Chemistry Chemistry of the nucleus Nuclear power, X-rays
Industrial Chemistry Large-scale production Cement, soap, fertilizer factories

💡 Chemistry All Around You

You don't need a lab to see chemistry — it's happening everywhere, right now:

  1. Cooking 🍳 — Frying an egg is a chemical change. The protein in the egg changes permanently when heated.
  2. Rusting 🔩 — That brown layer on an iron gate? Iron reacting with oxygen and water.
  3. Photosynthesis 🌱 — Plants use sunlight to convert CO₂ + water → sugar + oxygen.
  4. Medicine 💊 — Panadol works because of chemistry: it blocks the enzyme that causes pain signals.
  5. Battery 🔋 — Your phone is powered by chemical reactions inside the battery.
  6. Soap 🧼 — Soap molecules have one end attracted to oil and another to water, which is why they clean.

⚠️ Common Confusions — Read This Carefully

⚠️ Confusion #1: "Is air really matter? It feels like nothing!"

✅ Yes, air is matter! Here's the proof: take an empty balloon and weigh it. Now inflate it and weigh it again. The inflated balloon is heavier — because air has mass.

⚠️ Confusion #2: "Chemistry = just memorizing formulas"

❌ Wrong! Chemistry is mostly about understanding. Once you understand why something happens, the formulas come naturally. Don't memorize blindly — understand first.

⚠️ Confusion #3: "Organic = healthy/natural food (like organic vegetables)"

❌ In chemistry, "organic" means containing carbon atoms. It has nothing to do with farming or healthy food. Petrol and plastic are also "organic" in chemistry!

Bonus: Why Light is NOT Matter

Light is made of photons which have zero rest mass. It's energy, not matter. Here's a side-by-side comparison:

Property Stone 🪨 Light 💡
Can you weigh it? ✅ Yes ❌ No
Takes up space? ✅ Yes ❌ No
Has mass? ✅ Yes ❌ No
Is it matter? ✅ Yes ❌ No — it's energy
Key Takeaway

The universe has two things: Matter (stone, water, air, you — has mass) and Energy (light, heat, sound, electricity — no mass). Chemistry studies matter and how energy changes it.

Q4. Define matter and give two examples.

Answer: Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space (volume).
Examples: water (liquid), air (gas), iron (solid).

Q5. Name any three branches of chemistry and write one line about each.

Answer:
1. Organic Chemistry — studies carbon-containing compounds like petrol and sugar.
2. Inorganic Chemistry — studies compounds that do not contain carbon, like salt and metals.
3. Biochemistry — studies the chemical reactions happening inside living organisms.

Q6. Is light matter? Give a reason for your answer.

Answer: No, light is not matter. Matter must have mass and occupy space. Light (photons) has zero rest mass and does not occupy space — it is a form of energy.

📝 Summary

Key Points from This Lesson

  1. Chemistry = the study of what things are made of and how they change (composition, structure, properties, changes).
  2. Matter = anything with mass + volume. Air, water, and you are matter. Light and heat are energy — not matter.
  3. Chemistry has 7 main branches: Physical, Organic, Inorganic, Biochemistry, Analytical, Nuclear, Industrial — each focusing on a different area.
